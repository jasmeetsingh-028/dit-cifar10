Why EMA?

training weights are noisy from batch to batch,
EMA keeps a smoothed shadow copy that's used for sampling 
instead of the raw weights.


Setup: right after creating MODEL, create a second copy with identical architecture and identical starting weights.

model = DiT()
ema_model = copy.deepcopy(model)


Every training step, after the optimizer updates model's weights, 
 nudge ema_model's weights slightly toward model's current weights:


for ema_param, param in zip(ema_model.parameters(), model.parameters()):
    ema_param.data = decay * ema_param.data + (1 - decay) * param.data

With decay = 0.9999, each update only moves the EMA weights 0.01%
of the way toward the current training weights. 
It takes thousands of steps for the EMA to meaningfully shift: smoothing.


It's never part of the forward/backward pass during training 
it's purely a side computation, updated by this weighted-average
formula after each optimizer step, not by backprop.


One practical detail: EMA should skip the warmup period at
the very start of training, since the model's weights 
are essentially random garbage for the first several 
hundred steps


take home lesson: for first few (n steps) steps it keep the 
copy of model steps as weights updates are random at the 
beggining but ofter warmup for (i-n) steps the model weights 
are ema'ed to the ema model.
